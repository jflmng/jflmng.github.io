The model

Each food f has a dose df,k of each FODMAP group k, at a typical serving, in multiples of the low-FODMAP cut-off. In this demo the ratings stand in for grams: moderate = 1, high = 3.

Each person has six sensitivities sk ≥ 0, one per group, where sk = 1 / (dose of k that gives medium symptoms on its own).

Combined load (fructans, GOS, excess fructose, sorbitol, mannitol)Tf = Σk ∈ combined sk df,k
Lactose, on its ownAf = slac df,lac
Hurdle curveH(x) = xβ / (1 + xβ)
Chance of any symptomspf = 1 − (1 − b)(1 − H(Tf))(1 − H(Af))
Severity, given symptomslog Y = log m + γ log(Tf + Af + ε) + ηη ~ N(0, σ²)
Chance severe (above medium), given symptomsqf = Φ(γ log(Tf + Af + ε) / σ)
Priorsk ~ Half-Normal(0, τ²)
Each rating counts as w occasionslog posterior = w Σratings log-lik + log prior

A rating of fine has likelihood 1 − p, mild p(1 − q), severe pq. Each is raised to the power w, because “onion is fine” sums up many meals rather than one. Foods with no FODMAP load don’t move any sensitivity; they’re absorbed by the background rate b.

The hurdle is the zero part: below the line, most days nothing happens. β is the noise term on your threshold. Equivalently, symptoms occur when log T exceeds a logistic noise term with scale 1/β, so lower β means more day-to-day unpredictability.

τ = 0.5 puts the prior’s median sensitivity at about 0.34, so a sensitive person’s high serving lands near the medium-symptoms line. The Profile page shows the MAP estimate: the most likely sensitivities, found by projected gradient ascent with s ≥ 0.

How foods are sorted

The Predict page needs to know how sure the model is, so it draws 400 samples of the six sensitivities from the posterior (component-wise Metropolis, reflected at zero, started at the MAP, with a fixed seed so results don’t shift between visits). Each food’s chance of symptoms is averaged over the samples, with an 80% interval from the 10th to 90th percentile.

  • Unsure if the food is moderate or high in a group you haven’t rated any food for. These are left out.
  • Likely fine if the average is within 12 points of the background rate and the 90th percentile within 25.
  • Likely to cause problems, fine in small portions if a typical serving isn’t fine, but half or a quarter serving passes the same test. Doses scale with the portion.
  • Likely to cause problems, even in small portions if even the 10th percentile is 25 points above background and no smaller portion passes.
  • Anything else counts as unsure, with mixed evidence.

Porting notes

js/model.js is plain functions with no libraries — predict, logPost, grad, fit, sample, classify — and translates line for line to Kotlin.

The food data here is a demo subset in the same shape as the 200-entry reference: six group ratings per food. Swap in the full reference and map low → 0.2, moderate → 1, high → 3, unknown → 0.5 (flagged). Where a food is dominated by one group, typical serving ÷ low serving is a better dose estimate than the rating. Ratings are recalled rather than verified, so check them against the Monash app before relying on them.

For a diary mode later, sum doses over a meal window and feed them through the same equations — that’s where stacking in the combined load shows up.

A demo, not medical advice. Food ratings are approximate and unverified; check them against the Monash FODMAP app. Your ratings stay in this browser and aren’t sent anywhere.
